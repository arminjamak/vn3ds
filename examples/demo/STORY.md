# Snapshot: story bible

> **Terminology (latest, overrides anything below).**
> - **Snapshot worlds** are the original parallel worlds. There are many,
>   all running *at the same time*. Nobody, not the angels, the spirits, the
>   vampires or the Council, knows which one is the real one. They all act
>   on the **same shared data**, which is why a change in one ripples through
>   others. World 7,412 is one of them. The "one real Earth, captured
>   without knowing it" still exists in the lore, but *which* snapshot it is
>   is unknown even to the Council. Chapter 4 can play with that.
> - The snapshot worlds form a **coalition**: parallel lanes on the same
>   shared data. **Protection means Aron was pushed out of the coalition into
>   his own separate lane.** Nothing he does there feeds back into the shared
>   data; that was the point of moving him. (So when his mind leaks into the
>   protected world and, later, hints of it reach the coalition, that's
>   alarming to the entities, and it's what makes him an architect.)
> - **The "energy vampires" are not literal vampires.** Each is a cosmic
>   being that has *swallowed the simulations*: every world it governs
>   exists inside its own form. Lord Ashveil's elegant, pale, red-eyed
>   humanoid shape is a **symbol**, the approximation Aron's mind reaches for
>   because it can't imagine what created everything imagined. "Energy
>   vampire" is the closest true thing in Aron's worldview: something
>   that feeds on the life it contains and gives a reality back. Ashveil
>   knows this and can say it ("You see teeth because you expect teeth"). To
>   other minds (the spirits, the angels, the captured Earth that calls it
>   God) the same being appears differently. Keep the word "vampire" as
>   Aron's and the Council's shorthand, never as literal fangs and blood.
> - **Childlike and hungry.** For all their vastness these beings are
>   very childlike, motivated mostly by hunger. The words "angels",
>   "spirits", "the Purgatorium", "Lantern Watch" and "the Council" are
>   *their* names, given the way a child names its toys and games. This
>   contrasts with the cold process codenames of the superintelligence
>   underneath (LOOM, ORCHARD, MERIDIAN, TIDEWATER). Ashveil can be
>   petulant, delighted, bored, greedy for a new taste. His menace is a
>   child's, not a tyrant's, which makes his final choice to starve himself
>   all the more striking.
> - **Aron's tone (latest user direction, overrides "Aron" below where they
>   differ).** Aron is *not* paranoid and doesn't have a complete breakdown
>   out of his own anxiety. Questioning reality is a **hobby**, done in
>   **secret** while he moves through completely everyday scenarios:
>   grocery queues, coffee, work meetings, the bus, laundry, dinner with
>   Mira. He's calm, curious, wry, a little playful: "Let's see if the
>   cashier says 'have a good one' again." He keeps it light and private
>   (the notebook, Miso). The plot beats stay (ambulance, ward, diagnosis,
>   meds), but their cause shifts outward: he gets *too good* at his hobby, so
>   the system throttles him and his world glitches around him. To everyone
>   else that looks like a sudden breakdown. On the ward he stays mostly
>   himself, bemused and gentle rather than raving, and the restraint
>   follows a confusing, system-induced episode, not a frenzy. Keep both
>   readings (illness or system) possible, but write him as a sane, likeable
>   man with an odd hobby.
> - **The protected world** is Aron's quarantine: a copy of World 7,412 made
>   of pattern people, where he has been since the ambulance ride. Every
>   earlier use of "snapshot world" for his quarantine now means the
>   protected world. The entities call it "the protected world" or just
>   "protection".

A mystical visual novel in four chapters (about 2 hours each), told from
Aron's point of view, with cuts to the beings who run his world. The cuts
reveal the system piece by piece. Aron never sees them; the player does.

The player can't lose. Every route reaches an ending. Choices change how
Aron gets there, who he becomes and which ending he reaches.

## The world, from the top down

Reveal these in roughly this order, across the four chapters, mostly through
the cuts. Never dump it all at once.

1. **Simulations.** There is one real Earth. Every other world is a copy of it
   at some moment in history, run forward to explore decisions. Every
   meaningful decision forks a timeline. The search is for the *best* world,
   not a local minimum, so wild deviations are tolerated, even encouraged.
2. **Conscious beings are expensive.** Simulators want as few as possible in
   a fresh experiment in case it goes wrong. Worlds that prove stable earn
   more conscious beings, and those beings are meant to interact a lot so they
   learn from each other and grow. Aron half-intuits this from Nick Bostrom's
   simulation argument. Odds are we're simulated, and a careful simulator
   would also keep the conscious headcount low. *Is he alone?*
3. **The Angel corps.** Something like an army. Its work is to feed
   creativity and opportunity into worlds, and sometimes to remote-control
   less-conscious people so the world feels real to a test subject. It is run
   by **Commander Seraphiel**: precise, dutiful, not cruel, and increasingly
   unsettled.
4. **The spirits.** They are spawned and trained in **the Purgatorium**, which
   all worlds share. Spirits run the automatic things: breathing, blinking,
   the walk to the fridge, small talk ("kitchen talk"). They are the substance
   of the not-conscious humanoids. Sometimes a person is half conscious and
   half spirit-run, to test a system with a human drive. Our spirits are
   **Pim** (blue, chatty, runs kitchen talk on shift 7) and **Dross** (green,
   grumpy, runs automatic movement, has run Aron's neighbour's breathing for
   eleven years).
5. **Energy vampires.** Each world has a governing energy vampire, a galactic
   being whose secret real goal is to feed on humanity and live comfortably.
   In return it builds reality for its test subjects. Aron's world belongs to
   **Lord Ashveil**: elegant, patient, amused, hungry. The vampires form **the
   Council**, which decides the fate and direction of worlds.
6. **The disturbance.** Aron is a disturbance. A conscious subject who starts
   working out the rules is treated like a rogue process. The first response
   is **throttling**: Aron's energy is deliberately lowered, because a
   slowed-down rogue system does less harm. His exhaustion is intentional,
   and the medication fog is that policy made physical.
7. **Quarantine.** The Council moves Aron into a **protected world**. It copies
   his world 100%, but the inhabitants are simulated from pattern recognition
   instead of being agents of their own. This keeps the original world's data
   clean and lets that experiment continue on a simulated timeline. Aron's old
   world is marked infected, and he is set aside where nothing he does can
   matter. He doesn't know when it happened. **The player should not be told
   exactly when either, until chapter 3.**
8. **The Lantern Watch.** A secret few who watch Aron constantly: Ember (a
   warm, orange spirit with a lantern) and others, possibly including Pim
   later. Watching him with all his faults, they came to care, because they
   see he's a good man. They help in tiny deniable ways: a light that flickers
   three times, a song on the radio, a stranger who says the right sentence.
   They risk being decommissioned for it.
9. **The offer.** Eventually Ashveil notices Aron and offers him a transfer to
   another world. He won't say who there is real. He says only that it is
   *better*, and he can't say by what metric. He knows it cosmically, a kind of
   objective feeling.
10. **The deepest layer.** Behind everything a **superintelligence** is being
    built. It hides inside the different world-building processes, each under
    a codename (`LOOM`, `ORCHARD`, `MERIDIAN`, `TIDEWATER`). It secretly runs
    the whole simulation. The real Earth is a **captured planet** that doesn't
    know it. Its people experience the occupation as something spiritual, as
    God.
11. **The new order.** Aron sees through it and designs his own system, in
    which every sentient simulated being gets a fair share of its world. He
    becomes chief architect of a new world order. One energy vampire chooses
    to sacrifice itself by starving to death. That frees humanity to go its
    own way and keep the benefits of the occupation: interesting technology,
    life extension, and solace when people pray.

## Aron

Thirty. Kind, funny in a dad-joke way (see the tutorial), stubborn,
systematic. He keeps notebooks. He loves Mira. The story's spine:

- He wakes up one morning and starts to question whether the world is an
  illusion or something tangible.
- He works out the world's rules systematically, to the point that he starts
  to hear voices: otherworldly communication he can't place. He tries to
  decipher it and literally goes mad. He is hospitalised and **diagnosed with
  schizophrenia**.
- His meds make him extremely tired. Through ADHD stimulants, weed and street
  speed he sometimes breaks through the fog into sudden realisations. **Show
  the cost every time**: crashes, paranoia, Mira's fear, Theo's worry, his own
  shame. Never glamorise it, never give instructions.
- He makes understanding the world his secret life's mission.
- **The emotional arc:** he goes through real troubles and learns to connect
  emotionally even when he doesn't know whether the other person is real. He
  chooses for the sake of choosing, staying true to the kind of person he is
  *because he decided he's a person like that*. This is the thesis. The best
  endings reward it.

Handle the illness with respect. The story never settles whether he is
"really" ill or "really" seeing the system, and both readings must hold.
The medical staff are kind and competent. Mira is not a prop.

## Mira

Aron's girlfriend. Red hair, freckles, teal dress. She's warm, quick, a
little sarcastic, and a nurse's daughter who knows her way around hospitals.
She is conscious in the original world. **Interesting coincidences:** now and
then she reveals something she shouldn't know (a dream Aron never told
anyone, the exact words he wrote in his notebook). After the quarantine, the
Mira with Aron is a pattern-copy. Or is she? The story keeps that open until
the endings. At least one ending has the real Mira find her way to him.

## Shared motifs and facts (keep these consistent)

- **7:07.** Aron's clock always reads 7:07 when he wakes. The bird lands on
  the kitchen wire at 7:07 too.
- **The three birds** on the street wire, evenly spaced, always the same
  three.
- **The dream Mira shouldn't know:** a glass staircase that goes down into a
  calm sea, with a lighthouse at the bottom whose light blinks three times.
  Aron dreamt it at 19 and told no one. In ch1 Mira mentions "the lighthouse
  at the bottom of the stairs".
- **Three flickers** is the Lantern Watch's signal: a light blinks three
  times. It echoes the dream on purpose. Ember chose it.
- **The umbrella:** in ch1 Aron gives his umbrella to a soaked stranger on
  `bg night`, a not-conscious pattern person. That's why Ember starts
  watching him.
- **The notebook.** Its first line, written in ch1: *"If this is a test, I want
  to pass it by being kind."* It comes back in the last scene.
- **Dieter Rams.** Aron has a Braun-style alarm clock and quotes "less, but
  better" now and then from ch1 on. He's his favourite designer.
- **Theo's line:** "Bro, the universe doesn't run on your schedule." Later it
  turns out to be exactly the kind of line Pim writes.
- **The voices** speak in fragments that later turn out to be process logs:
  "...throttle approved...", "...subject A-30...", "...LOOM sync at
  dawn...". Write them through `vox` with `audio/voices.ogg`.
- **Aron's ID** in every cut is `Subject A-30`. His world is `World 7,412`.
- **The quarantine move happens during the ambulance ride at the end of
  chapter 1** (user decision; this overrides anything else in this bible
  about the rooftop). The entities transfer him while he's half-conscious on
  the stretcher. A cut confirms "transfer complete" as the ambulance arrives.
  **Chapters 2 and 3 take place entirely in the protected world.** The player
  only gets a hint; it's revealed clearly in chapter 3. The rooftop night at
  the end of chapter 2 stays as a key emotional scene (the three flickers,
  the decision to make this his secret life's mission), but it is not the
  transfer.
- **The leak (central mechanic of chapters 2–3).** Aron has to figure out
  whether he's really ill or whether something sinister is going on. Because
  his mind is inside a world built only from pattern copies, his mental state
  leaks into it. The snapshot functions weirder and weirder, quasi
  malfunctioning, and each glitch reveals a bit more of the system. Examples:
  - he gets into a cab and the driver explains that someone he knows, with
    Aron's exact name, went mentally ill the same way, out of the blue, just
    after getting into this cab
  - a table at a restaurant says one of *his* words, like "protected", a
    fragment of the system he's been working out
  - a radio host reads a line from his notebook
  - strangers say "subject" or "A-30" in passing
  - the same three birds appear indoors
  Escalate across chapters 2 and 3 from subtle to undeniable. Both readings
  (illness or system) must still hold until chapter 4.
- **The real Mira** stays in World 7,412, where Aron's body is "in a coma"
  (the original timeline's explanation). In ch3 a cut shows her by the
  hospital bed. In ch4's best ending she crosses over with help from the
  Watch.
- **Ashveil's offer** (end of ch3), in his words: "A better world, Aron. I
  can't tell you better *how*. I only know it the way you know warmth. And I
  won't tell you who there is real. That's the price of the ticket."

## Chapters

Each chapter is about 2 hours, roughly 1,800 to 2,400 lines including
branches. A player sees about 1,500 of them. Each has 10 to 16 choices, 4 to 6
cuts to the entities, and its own title card.

### Chapter 1 - "The Morning Render" (`label chapter1`, file `ch1*.rpy`)

The tutorial turns out to have been a calibration. Aron wakes at 7:07, and
small wrongnesses pile up: the bird on the wire, the coffee machine's sound,
three identical birds, the same sentence from a stranger. He thinks through
Bostrom. Mira reveals a dream he never told her. Theo laughs it off. Aron
starts the notebook and sets systematic tests (repeat when unobserved? the
7:07 rule?), and the player picks his methods. Voices begin. He tries to
decode them, gets obsessive, stops sleeping, and has a frightening episode
(the mirror, the rain). It ends with Mira and Theo taking him to hospital.

Cuts: Seraphiel's ops room (the angel corps, feeding opportunity, remote
control); Pim and Dross on a kitchen-talk shift that includes Mira's small
talk ("was that line yours or hers?"); Seraphiel flagging the disturbance; the
order to **throttle** (lower his energy, "a slowed-down rogue system does less
harm"); last of all, a first glimpse of Ember in the attic: "He gave his
umbrella to a stranger who didn't exist. Who does that?"

### Chapter 2 - "Fog" (`label chapter2`, file `ch2*.rpy`)

The ward. Dr. Okafor, Nurse Hale. The diagnosis is schizophrenia, and the
medication brings crushing tiredness. Mira's visits get harder. Group therapy,
breathing exercises, the boredom. Discharge. Life in the fog: lost job hours,
the bed. Theo, stimulants, weed, a night with street speed. Each time there
are sudden realisations and then the cost. The player chooses how far he goes;
there is always a gentler route and no route ends the game. Aron decides
understanding this is his **secret life's mission**, and chooses whether to
tell Mira. He learns to connect: one scene where he decides to love someone
without knowing whether they're real.

Cuts: the Council meets (Ashveil and the others, feeding, politics) and
argues over the quarantine that has *already* happened; Seraphiel's unease
about it; Pim and Dross trying to patch the protected world as Aron's mind leaks
into it ("why did the cab driver say his name?"); the Lantern Watch's first
deliberate help (a light flickers three times at exactly the moment he needs
a sign). **This chapter is already in the protected world, and the leak
glitches start here.**

### Chapter 3 - "Snapshot" (`label chapter3`, file `ch3*.rpy`)

Aron has been in the protected world since the ambulance ride, without
knowing it. Things are subtly smoother and more predictable, conversations
loop, Mira is lovely but sometimes *too* right, and the leak glitches become
undeniable. It is revealed that the move happened in the ambulance at the end
of chapter 1.
He keeps investigating with whatever energy he has. His troubles: a relapse
scare, a fight with Mira, Theo's own crisis, which Aron helps with even
though Theo may be a pattern. Aron chooses kindness for its own sake. The
Lantern Watch helps more boldly and gets caught, and Ember is put on trial.
Ashveil grows curious. The chapter ends with the **offer**: Ashveil appears to
Aron directly and offers a transfer to a better world. He won't say who's
real, or better by what metric.

Cuts: the protected world's machinery (pattern people being "rendered"), the
original world going on without Aron (the real Mira grieving or searching?),
Ember's trial, the Council arguing about Ashveil, first hints of the codenamed
processes `LOOM` and `ORCHARD`.

### Chapter 4 - "Chief Architect" (`label chapter4`, file `ch4*.rpy`)

The answer to the offer (choice). Whichever way he goes, Aron climbs toward
the deepest layer: the superintelligence wearing world-building codenames,
the captured real Earth (`bg earth`) worshipping its occupier as God, and the
Council's true appetite. Aron designs his own system on the blueprint, in
which every sentient simulated being gets a fair share of its world. Lord
Ashveil, or another vampire depending on the route (only Ashveil has a
sprite), chooses to starve itself, freeing humanity with the occupation's
benefits: technology, life extension, solace in prayer. The Lantern Watch's
fate. Mira's truth.

#### Endings (all written; none is a failure)

Choose the ending in `label ch4_ending_select` from the story variables:

- **"The Architect"** (needs high `clarity` and `integrity`): Aron becomes
  chief architect of the new order. Ashveil starves himself, and the real
  Earth is freed with its gifts. Mira (the real one) finds him. This is the
  true ending.
- **"Chosen"** (high `bond`): Aron refuses the system's game and chooses Mira,
  real or not, for the sake of choosing. Quiet and warm. A last cut hints she
  was real all along, or that it doesn't matter.
- **"Lantern"** (high `watch`): Aron joins the Lantern Watch and becomes the
  one who flickers lights for the next disturbance.
- **"The Long Fog"** (high `risky` / `fog`, low `integrity`): bittersweet. He
  lives a small, medicated, good life, and the Watch makes sure he never
  feels alone. Still not a loss: the final cut shows his notebook seeded the
  architect who comes after him.
- **"Snapshot"** (fallback): he stays in the copy and lives kindly anyway. The
  entities admit his kindness was real even if nothing around him was.

#### Seven endings: seven multi-world systems

The ending is chosen by the principle with the highest **weighted score**.
Its cousins count at half weight:

`score_X = 2 * abs(p_X) + abs(cousin1) + abs(cousin2)`

| principle | cousins |
|---|---|
| truth | exit, govern |
| share | spirits, govern |
| rest | less, spirits |
| spirits | share, rest |
| govern | truth, share |
| exit | truth, less |
| less | rest, exit |

On a tie, the order is less, truth, share, rest, spirits, govern, exit. If
everything is 0, the ending is `less` as an open question.

The winning principle's **sign** sets the flavour of its system, so each of
the seven endings has two versions. Each is a fully realised multi-world
system that Aron presents in `ch4_office`. The other six principles appear
as its supporting rules. The meter-based routes above (Architect, Chosen,
Lantern, Long Fog, Snapshot) are now the *road* into the office, not the
ending itself.

#### Coda (after every ending: `label ch4_coda`)

After the office presentation every route plays the same coda (with small
variations):

1. The council erupts in ovation.
2. Then everything goes on like a usual day. Aron makes coffee; it's 7:07;
   the bird lands. It's ordinary, and that's the point.
3. It turns out there are worlds upon worlds upon worlds (`bg worlds`). His
   changes ripple only through one subsection of the whole organism. Short
   scenes show other worlds' architects fighting and arguing for their
   worlds: two or three nameless voices in other councils (`bg council`,
   `bg command`, `bg office`), each pitching a different system.
4. The reveal (Seraphiel, Ember or Ashveil's last words, depending on route):
   finding **world-architect beings** was the goal all along: beings who can
   translate the complex into simple, efficient systems intuitively, relying
   on logic and emotion together, shaping reality at least in their heads for
   now.
5. The secret goal: to give such beings the **light technique**, the science
   of creating matter out of nothing with beam-like golden energy
   (`bg goldlight`). That light becomes the new coat of the elevated being
   (`aron light smile/awe/normal`). They become architects of worlds
   eternally, which is their ultimate reward.
6. A last quiet beat with Mira (or her absence, or the Watch), then `return`.

#### The ultimate twist (`label ch4_record`, the very end of every route)

Everything the player has seen, tutorial included, was **a record**: the
archived life of a being who challenged the system, achieved everything and
received the ultimate reward. Now look at the reward honestly. It is an
existence of **solitude**, a frenzy of endless creation in which he forgot
every other human part of existence: Mira's laugh, Theo's terrible jokes,
coffee at 7:07, being tired next to someone.

- Frame it as the record closing. An archivist (Pim, now old and promoted)
  files it: `Subject A-30. Architect. Status: eternal. Companions: none.`
  Then the camera finds the golden figure (`aron light normal` on
  `bg goldlight` or `bg worlds`), alone, building worlds without stopping,
  without looking up.
- Make it bittersweet, not cruel. The player should feel the cost of the
  maximum.
- Vary the last few lines on `bond` and the route, without undoing the
  twist. On high-`bond` routes, the light-figure pauses for one heartbeat as
  if remembering a red-haired woman and the word *lighthouse*, then goes on
  building. On the Chosen route the archivist adds a footnote: "In one
  branch he put down the light. That record is not stored here."
- The final line echoes the tutorial, e.g. the archivist asking an empty
  room, "Did the birds just stop moving?" Then `return`.

Order: route ending, then `ch4_office` (the system presentation), then
`ch4_coda`, then `ch4_record`, then `return`.

#### The hidden design (the player builds it without knowing)

`common.rpy` defines seven principle variables, `p_truth p_share p_rest
p_spirits p_govern p_exit p_less`. The comments there say what + and - mean.
**Every chapter must include at least one choice that moves each of the
seven** (`$ p_rest += 1` and so on). Hide them inside ordinary story choices,
so the player is secretly working out principles through what Aron does:

- Telling Mira the truth vs. sparing her: `p_truth`.
- Sharing his last good hour with Theo vs. using it on his research:
  `p_share`.
- Resting vs. pushing through on stimulants: `p_rest`.
- Thanking the "stranger" who may be a pattern person, or treating the
  spirits as people: `p_spirits`.
- Asking others before acting vs. quietly acting alone: `p_govern`.
- Wanting to escape vs. wanting to fix things where he is: `p_exit`.
- Fixing one small thing well vs. a grand plan: `p_less`.

A choice may move two principles, or one principle and one story meter. Never
tell the player a principle changed. The final scene makes it legible.

#### The last scene (every route ends here)

After its ending, every route plays **`label ch4_office`**: the final scene,
shared by all routes and shaped by the story variables.

Aron stands in a bright architectural office in a *perfect* world
(`bg office`), presenting to cosmic beings of a different order. Ashveil is
present on some routes, the Watch on others. He explains which structural
changes the network running existence needs. That is how it earns its place
among them and the right to run its own experiments.

From now on his job is to work towards the best guiding principles, aiming
at the absolute maximum rather than a local minimum, while staying true to
his favourite designer, **Dieter Rams**: *"Less, but better."* Paraphrase
Rams's design principles (useful, honest, unobtrusive, long-lasting,
thorough down to the last detail, as little design as possible) and apply
them to worlds. Don't quote them at length.

The decisions show up here in several ways:
- which principles he puts first (fair share for every sentient being? the
  right to know who is real? rest for throttled minds? mercy for the spirits?)
- who stands beside him (Mira, the real one or the copy, or nobody yet;
  Ember; Theo)
- whether the Council listens or argues
- what he says about the notebook from chapter 1

Use `if` blocks on `clarity`, `bond`, `integrity`, `watch`, `fog`, `risky`
and the flags to vary 30–60% of the scene's lines.

**The presentation is the player's design.** Aron explains to the Council of
cosmic beings, in detail, the system his choices built. For each of the seven
principles write three versions: positive, negative, and an "open question"
for 0. Each version is 6 to 12 lines of Aron explaining it concretely: how it
works, what it costs, which moment of his life taught it (quote back the
choice that set it). Have a cosmic being push back on at least two of them,
and let Aron answer. Then **name the system** from its two strongest
principles (e.g. *"The Open Lighthouse"*, *"The Quiet Garden"*,
*"The Parliament of Rest"*); write at least 8 names, picked with `if`/`elif`
on the leanings. Every combination must read as a coherent, different world.
The Council's verdict is always to admit the network, with a different
reason each time. End on a quiet, hopeful image and then `return`.

## Writing rules for this engine (important)

- Read `README.md` → "What converts". Use only that subset. In particular:
  - no screens, no Python functions, lists or dicts
  - `$` lines only for simple assignments and arithmetic (`$ clarity += 1`)
  - no `renpy.input`, no `nvl`, no layeredimage
  - text tags are stripped, so don't rely on `{b}` or `{color}`

  `scene black with blackout` then `centered "Chapter N\n\nTitle"`.

**72 scenes are available, all stereoscopic and layered, many animated:**

`pov ...` scenes are first-person, literally looking out of Aron's head. They
have a vignette, the blurred edge of his nose and heavy eyelids. Use them
whenever he questions existence, examines his hands, stares at the ceiling at
7:07, looks up at the three birds, meets his reflection, or stares into his
coffee. `cg ...` scenes are key-moment illustrations.

**Camera rules:** change scenes often. A typical stretch of 40–60 lines should
use 2–4 scenes. Cut to a different angle of the same place to mark a shift in
thought or mood: `bg kitchen` to `bg kitchen table` to `pov cup`, `bg ward` to
`bg ward window` to `bg corridor`, `bg rooftop` to `bg rooftop edge` to
`pov sky`, `bg cafe` to `bg cafe window`, `bg bedroom` to `bg bedroom door` to
`pov ceiling`. Use the time and weather variants (`bg street rain/dusk/snow`,
`bg park night/autumn`, `bg bedroom night`, `bg kitchen night`,
`bg room night`, `bg ward night`, `bg rooftop dawn`) to show time passing.

**Miso**, Aron's cat (`cat`, sprites `miso sit/stare/meow/loaf`, transform
`catspot`). She's an orange tabby with a white chest, and she is the running
gag. At home Aron sometimes explains his elaborate theories to her, at length,
seriously and in great detail. At the end she says `cat "Meow."`, and that's
always the end of the conversation. Never break that rule: nothing follows
her meow in that conversation; cut to the next scene. Use it 2–4 times per
chapter. It's funny, and as the story darkens it becomes tender. (Who looks
after Miso during the ward and quarantine is up to each chapter; keep her
alive and well in all routes.)
